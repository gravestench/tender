# ACH WEB mandate qualification for the Adyen candidate

Checked against official Adyen and Nacha material on 2026-11-24. This note describes a proposed **one-time US consumer ACH debit** in the existing Adyen Checkout v72 Sessions / Adyen Web 6.45.2 path. It does not qualify a merchant, approve legal language, or enable SANDBOX or LIVE ACH. The MOCK checkout's synthetic consent is only a fixture. See [the ACH setup guide](providers/ach.md) and [current evidence](evidence/ach.md).

## What the merchant must approve

Adyen requires a mandate **before** a debit is initiated. It says the authorization identifies the bank account, transaction type, frequency, amount and revocation method, and is electronically signed. For a consumer WEB debit, Nacha says the authorization must be clearly identifiable and understandable; its proof guide identifies express debit authority, amount, date/frequency, account number, routing number, and revocation terms for recurring or advance-scheduled debits. Nacha does **not** prescribe one exact sentence or format. Its sample language explicitly calls for legal review. The merchant, its ODFI/Adyen and counsel must approve the final copy and signature method for Blacktop's actual order, funding timing, cancellation channel and account-linkage arrangement. [Adyen ACH mandates](https://docs.adyen.com/payment-methods/ach-direct-debit/) · [Nacha WEB proof guide, pp. 3–6](https://www.nacha.org/system/files/2022-11/WEB_Proof_of_Authorization_Industry_Practices.pdf) · [Nacha authorization guidance](https://www.nacha.org/news/importance-compliant-ach-authorizations)

Candidate **copy for review, not deployable legal text** (render actual values, never the braces):

> I authorize **{Blacktop legal originator name}** to initiate **one ACH debit** of **{USD amount}** from the **{checking or savings} account entered above** for **order {order ID}**, to be submitted **{specific date or clearly defined timing}**. I understand that this is a single payment, not permission for future debits. To ask about this authorization or request cancellation before submission, contact **{contact method}** by **{applicable cutoff and time zone}**. By selecting **Authorize one ACH debit**, I electronically sign this authorization.

Approval must settle the visible legal originator name and statement descriptor, whether the named account/routing details must appear in the authorization or customer copy, the exact debit date/cutoff, account-owner authentication, the cancellation procedure, and any provider-required words. The authorization presented to the shopper must be bound to the **particular account entered**, rather than merely an unrelated checkbox. Nacha's sample permits an account-on-record description only when the account is retained; whether a retrievable Adyen-held account record and reference meet this merchant's proof obligation is **unconfirmed**. No general “future debits” grant should be inferred from this one-time text. [Nacha WEB proof guide, pp. 3–6](https://www.nacha.org/system/files/2022-11/WEB_Proof_of_Authorization_Industry_Practices.pdf)

Use an unchecked, explicit authorization control and a separate, clearly labeled final button. Record the exact rendered terms (or immutable version plus rendered variables and digest), UTC signature time, authenticated customer/session identity and authentication method, trusted order/payment/session/merchant IDs, amount and currency, assent action, and the approved account reference. Preserve the proof process, not merely a screenshot or a bare checkbox: Nacha says a screenshot alone does not link the authorization to the account holder, and examples include login time, IP audit data, or another identity link. Decide the minimum identity/audit data with the merchant's privacy counsel and processor. [Nacha WEB proof guide, pp. 3–4](https://www.nacha.org/system/files/2022-11/WEB_Proof_of_Authorization_Industry_Practices.pdf)

Nacha's guide states that the originator must retain an accurately reproducible authorization and its link to the consumer for **two years from termination or revocation**, and provide it to the ODFI on request. Nacha also says to give the consumer a copy for their records. Blacktop needs a durable, access-controlled proof record, a customer-readable copy, an actual retrieval/export procedure, and a defined retention start/end event for this single debit. Merchant counsel and the processor must approve any stricter contractual or legal period and the handling of bank identifiers in those copies. [Nacha WEB proof guide, p. 4](https://www.nacha.org/system/files/2022-11/WEB_Proof_of_Authorization_Industry_Practices.pdf) · [Nacha authorization guidance](https://www.nacha.org/news/importance-compliant-ach-authorizations)

## Same-page boundary in pinned Adyen Web 6.45.2

The pinned ACH form renders account type, owner name, routing number, account number and verification as `InputText` fields, and optionally renders its pay button. Its `formatData()` assembles raw routing/account values. The form has **no mandate text, authorization checkbox or signature evidence**. This is an inspection of the pinned source, not a claim about later SDK versions. [ACH form](https://github.com/Adyen/adyen-web/blob/v6.45.2/packages/lib/src/components/Ach/components/AchComponent.tsx) · [ACH data](https://github.com/Adyen/adyen-web/blob/v6.45.2/packages/lib/src/components/Ach/Ach.tsx)

A feasible page sequence for **further qualification** is:

1. Tender creates the amount-bound session from the trusted order. The checkout page presents the Adyen `Ach` Component with `showPayButton: false`, `hasHolderName: true`, no stored-details option, and the merchant's terms/unchecked consent control on the **same page**. Do not use an Advanced-flow `onSubmit`, `onChange`, `onReview` or `beforeSubmit` callback for this ACH component: the pinned SDK can hand such callbacks the raw payment data. [Pinned ACH form](https://github.com/Adyen/adyen-web/blob/v6.45.2/packages/lib/src/components/Ach/components/AchComponent.tsx) · [Pinned submit path](https://github.com/Adyen/adyen-web/blob/v6.45.2/packages/lib/src/components/internal/UIElement/UIElement.tsx)
2. The customer reads the mandate and affirmatively signs with the unchecked control and merchant button. The host posts **only non-bank consent evidence** to its server, verifies the customer/order/session/amount and stores a pending proof record. Only after a successful acknowledgement does the button call `achComponent.submit()`; no page navigation or form post is needed. The approved page must prevent further edits between assent and SDK submission, or re-obtain assent if any payment detail changes. A failed/expired consent blocks the ordinary UI path. The pinned `submit()` validates the SDK form and, with no merchant payment callbacks, uses Sessions. The Sessions transport posts the payment request directly to Adyen. [Pinned submit path](https://github.com/Adyen/adyen-web/blob/v6.45.2/packages/lib/src/components/internal/UIElement/UIElement.tsx) · [Sessions transport](https://github.com/Adyen/adyen-web/blob/v6.45.2/packages/lib/src/core/Services/sessions/make-payment.ts)
3. On SDK completion or failure, send only the opaque `sessionResult` to Tender for the authenticated session-result exchange. Bind the pending mandate to the resulting PSP/verified event. If the browser closes or the result is lost, retain the pending proof for reconciliation until provider evidence establishes whether a debit occurred; never discard it solely because the browser session expired. A pending Adyen result is not settled funds. Deliver the customer copy through an approved channel and retain a retrieval pointer with the payment. [Adyen Sessions guide](https://docs.adyen.com/online-payments/build-your-integration/sessions-flow/) · [Adyen ACH payment risk](https://docs.adyen.com/payment-methods/ach-direct-debit/)

This sequence keeps bank numbers out of **Tender Go requests, logs and storage** if the page and callbacks obey it. It does **not** provide a technical guarantee that arbitrary first-party JavaScript cannot read the numbers: the pinned ACH fields are ordinary inputs in the checkout document, and the SDK itself holds the values. A strong “host JS cannot access bank data” claim requires a different, independently verified isolation surface. No such surface is established here. For this candidate, host code must not inspect those inputs or the component's `data`/`state`/`formatData()`, attach form-state callbacks, serialize the page, log request bodies, or send the numbers to Blacktop. This is a code and browser-audit constraint, not an SDK-enforced secrecy boundary. [Pinned ACH form](https://github.com/Adyen/adyen-web/blob/v6.45.2/packages/lib/src/components/Ach/components/AchComponent.tsx) · [Pinned ACH data](https://github.com/Adyen/adyen-web/blob/v6.45.2/packages/lib/src/components/Ach/Ach.tsx)

The consent check is also only a page control. Once a payable Sessions credential is in the browser, a modified client could call Adyen directly without invoking the merchant's button; the merchant server cannot intercept the provider's direct payment POST. Qualify a provider/ODFI accepted enforcement and evidence model for this risk. A happy-path browser test must not be treated as proof that every provider debit has a mandate. [Pinned Sessions transport](https://github.com/Adyen/adyen-web/blob/v6.45.2/packages/lib/src/core/Services/sessions/make-payment.ts)

The unresolved proof problem is important: Nacha's WEB guide lists account and routing numbers in the authorization evidence, while this design intentionally excludes them from merchant systems. Before enabling real debits, obtain **written Adyen/ODFI acceptance** of a provider-held account reference, evidence/copy retrieval method, and retention/production procedure that can connect the customer's specific entered account to the signed mandate without putting raw bank numbers into Tender. An opaque session ID or PSP alone is not assumed sufficient. [Nacha WEB proof guide, pp. 3–4](https://www.nacha.org/system/files/2022-11/WEB_Proof_of_Authorization_Industry_Practices.pdf)

## Qualification evidence to add to the protected sandbox path

The current `test/sandbox/ach-browser.cjs` checks that the pinned browser submits published giact fixtures to Adyen, stays on one page, and does not transmit fixture numbers to the merchant origin. It mounts the SDK's own pay button and **does not test a real mandate**. `TestACHSandboxSessions` ends in `BLOCKED` even after a provider response. The MOCK consumer displays synthetic consent without bank details, so it cannot satisfy the real mandate gate. [Adyen giact setup and test cases](https://docs.adyen.com/payment-methods/ach-direct-debit/verification-with-giact/)

Extend that credentialed test to verify: approved versioned terms and transaction details visible on the same page; unchecked consent prevents any `/sessions/{id}/payments` request; server-acknowledged consent is durable before `component.submit()`; exactly one direct provider payment POST occurs after assent; no host request, telemetry, URL, console or stored proof contains the test routing/account values; no popup, top-level redirect or unexpected destination occurs; and a customer copy plus retrievable merchant record match the signed amount, order, session and provider account linkage. Test refusal, timeout/abandonment and duplicate click alongside a pass. Correlate the resulting signed event and authenticated settlement/return report with the same order. Use sanitized identifiers in CI output; keep the raw proof and any provider-exported bank data in approved restricted storage. A browser pass on synthetic fixtures establishes transport and UI behavior only, not legal sufficiency or LIVE eligibility. [Adyen giact setup and test cases](https://docs.adyen.com/payment-methods/ach-direct-debit/verification-with-giact/) · [Nacha WEB proof guide](https://www.nacha.org/system/files/2022-11/WEB_Proof_of_Authorization_Industry_Practices.pdf)

## Enablement decision

Keep ordinary `NewACH` SANDBOX/LIVE capabilities at zero until the merchant has: activated ACH and giact consumer ecommerce validation for its account; approved the exact terms and e-sign process with counsel/processor; confirmed provider-held account proof and retention/copy retrieval; audited the same-page JS and browser network boundary; and observed an actual credentialed test debit, refusal, signed notification, settlement and return evidence. Adyen says its Components handle the WEB SEC code and giact can validate first-use consumer accounts when the merchant signs the giact addendum and Support enables the option. A giact pass does not guarantee funds or merchant acceptance, and this integration reaches eligible US institutions **through Adyen**, not through independent bank integrations. [Adyen ACH](https://docs.adyen.com/payment-methods/ach-direct-debit/) · [Adyen giact](https://docs.adyen.com/payment-methods/ach-direct-debit/verification-with-giact/)
